TECHNOLOGY — THE ENGINE

Under the hood

Orbit does not chain keystrokes. It reads a full expression the way maths is written, evaluates it with a real grammar, and keeps the whole session on the device.

A real expression grammar

The engine is a two-stage pipeline: a lexer turns the input into numbers, names, operators and parentheses; a recursive-descent parser builds the expression with explicit precedence. That means 2 + 3 × 4 is fourteen, 2(3 + 4) is fourteen through implicit multiplication, and 2^3^2 is right-associative. Parentheses, unary signs, postfix factorial and percent all parse without special-casing the keypad.

Function names resolve against a fixed table: sin, cos, tan, inverse trig, ln, log, sqrt, cbrt, abs and exp, plus the constants π and e. Trigonometry respects the DEG/RAD switch, including exact quadrant results: sin 180° evaluates to zero, not 1.2E-16.

Bounded and honest evaluation

Every expression is length- and nesting-bounded before evaluation. Domain errors are reported as readable messages: division by zero, a factorial outside 0–170, a logarithm of a non-positive number, a non-finite result. Nothing silently becomes NaN on the display. Results keep full precision internally and are formatted at the edge, so chained calculations never accumulate rounding in the visible number.

Number formatting

Display precision is adjustable from 4 to 12 decimals. Optional digit grouping is display-only; the stored value is untouched. Very large or very small magnitudes switch to scientific notation. Copying a result copies the formatted value; reusing it keeps the full-precision internal value.

History and memory

Every committed result is appended to a 100-entry history with its expression. The list is searchable, and each entry can be reused as an expression, inserted as a value, copied or deleted. Memory (MC, MR, M+, M−, MS) is a separate register that survives restarts alongside history and settings. All of it is local JSON in app storage.

Converters

Nine categories — length, weight, temperature, area, volume, speed, time, data and energy — share one unit catalog. Temperature uses affine conversion with absolute-zero validation rather than a simple ratio. Data units include both decimal and binary definitions, and a one-tap swap keeps the direction obvious.

Exact money maths

The bill splitter never uses binary floating point for money. Amounts are parsed as BigDecimal, the tip is applied at full precision, and each share is rounded with CEILING so the collected total always covers the bill. The displayed currency label is a label; the maths is currency-agnostic.

Private by construction

Orbit's manifest has no internet permission. There is no analytics SDK, no ad SDK, no account system and no remote configuration. History, memory and settings are excluded from Android backup and device transfer. The app cannot send a number anywhere because it has no network capability to do so.

Parser

Lexer + recursive descent, precedence, implicit multiplication, postfix operations, bounded nesting.

Precision

Full-precision internal results, formatting at the display edge, DEG/RAD aware trigonometry.

Money

BigDecimal totals, percentage tips, ceiling shares, exact coverage of the bill.

Privacy

No network permission, no accounts, no ads, local storage only.
